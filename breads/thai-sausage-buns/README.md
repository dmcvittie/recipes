# Thai Sweet Sausage Buns 🌭🍞

Soft, slightly sweet milk-bread dough wrapped around hot dogs — the kind you see in Thai bakeries. Knead by hand for about 5 minutes; the buns stay soft all day.

Source: [Krua Maenai — Sausage Bread (YouTube)](https://youtu.be/ImNXAG2cNuw)

Makes 10-12 buns.

## Dough

- 300 g bread flour
- 1 1/2 teaspoons instant yeast
- 50 g granulated sugar
- 1/2 teaspoon salt
- 160 ml warm milk (warm to the touch, not hot)
- 1 egg
- 50 g unsalted butter (or margarine), softened
- A little oil for greasing (optional — I skip it)

## Filling + Topping

- 10-12 hot dogs (I used Schneiders Red Hots)
- 1 egg + a splash of water, beaten (egg wash)

## Directions

1. In a large bowl, mix the flour, yeast, sugar and salt.
2. Add the warm milk and egg, and mix until a shaggy dough forms.
3. Add the softened butter and knead by hand for about 5 minutes, until the dough is smooth and soft. It will be a bit sticky at first — keep going rather than adding more flour.
4. Shape into a ball, cover, and let rise somewhere warm until roughly doubled, about 45-60 minutes.
5. Punch the dough down and divide into 10-12 equal pieces (about 50 g each — weigh them for even buns). Roll into balls, cover, and rest 10 minutes.
6. Roll each ball into a long thin rope, long enough to spiral around a hot dog.
7. Pat the hot dogs dry, then wrap a rope around each one in a spiral from end to end, tucking the ends underneath.
8. Place on a parchment-lined baking sheet with space between them. Cover and let rise until puffy, about 30-45 minutes.
9. Preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C) while they rise.
10. Brush gently with the egg wash.
11. Bake 15-18 minutes, until deep golden brown.
12. Cool on a rack for a few minutes before eating.

### Notes

- **A)** The video's English ingredient list has a stray "50 g Salt" line — that's a typo. Only 1/2 teaspoon of salt goes in.
- **B)** Rise and bake times above are typical for this style of soft bread; the video's description doesn't list them, so adjust after the first batch and update this recipe.
- **C)** Full-size hot dogs are longer than the cocktail sausages Thai bakeries use. If the ropes come up short, cut the hot dogs in half and make twice as many smaller buns.
- **D)** Great with coffee or tea, and the kids love them.
